BORDER SERVICES GROUP (FB) - APPLICATION GUIDELINES
================================================================================

PURPOSE: This Application Guidelines document has been developed to assist evaluators in understanding and applying the Border Services classification standard to accurately, fairly and consistently evaluate Border Services work. This is a reference tool and must be used in conjunction with the Border Services classification standard. In the case of discrepancy between the standard and the guidelines, the standard will prevail.

OVERVIEW OF CLASSIFICATION:
- Classification establishes the relative value of jobs based on objective, fair, consistent, and gender-neutral measurement.
- Classification does NOT consider the performance or seniority of individual incumbents.
- The Border Services standard covers four Pay Equity factors: Skills, Responsibility, Effort and Working Conditions.
- Standard uses a point-rating system.

KEY GUIDING PRINCIPLES FOR RATERS:
1. Jobs not People - evaluate the work required of the subject position, not an incumbent's ability to perform it.
2. Typical Ongoing Job Responsibilities - select the statement describing the HIGHEST level the job is TYPICALLY expected to work and that SIGNIFICANTLY represents ongoing responsibilities. Do NOT consider exceptional, one-time circumstances or developmental/stretch opportunities.

THE STRUCTURE OF EACH ELEMENT:
Each element includes:
- General guidelines (definition and design intent of the element)
- Specific guidelines (structure and mechanics; clarification of each degree; how to differentiate among degrees)
- Definitions (specific terminology)
- Examples of Work Activities (EWAs) - not exhaustive but provide indicators of what kinds and levels of work should be assigned to a given degree

ELEMENT-BY-ELEMENT GUIDANCE:

ELEMENT 1 - KNOWLEDGE (application guidelines):
At D1: Focus of knowledge is on specific work area with well-defined range of duties. Some specific, short-term practical training may be required; knowledge is not heavily experience-based nor does it require understanding of Agency business or contextual environment surrounding the work.
At D2: Growing in nature, requiring working knowledge of techniques and practices associated with a given area of expertise. Basic understanding of the broader operational and legislative context.
At D3: Increases in depth from D2 to deliver on higher levels of accountability, responsibility, compliance and service. Understanding of technical concepts and principles, strong understanding of legislation and regulations and their application in a variety of situations, knowledge of inter-relationships of operations and mandates.
At D4: Domain knowledge depth increases to advanced level with knowledge of trends, theories and practices. Understanding of trends and patterns of the broader context; restricted in scope to services and programs; thorough knowledge of professional techniques.
At D5: Depth in specialized field grows further to advanced level; breadth of knowledge grows significantly to include thorough understanding of CBSA's business climate; broader knowledge of services and programs and their overall impact on other Agency business delivery strategies; knowledge of operational management approaches.
At D6: Extensive knowledge and business know-how to lead the organization in strategic activities; directing a varied and significant subordinate organization; paramount knowledge and appreciation of the external environment of the Agency.

ELEMENT 2 - ANALYTICAL SKILLS (application guidelines):
Cumulative progression ("pyramid") element - higher degree assumes lower degrees are included.
At D1: Reviewing and processing information using well-defined processes or procedures within prescribed guidelines.
At D2: Assessing information requirements given specific situations or circumstances and guidelines; gathering additional information or obtaining data.
At D3: Compiling and synthesizing information from a variety and diversity of sources to form a cohesive picture; judgment required in determining best course of action.
At D4: Identifying patterns, trends and inconsistencies; study and evaluation of previous level analyses; issues/situations for focus can be elusive or unclear; creativity and study required.
At D5: Investigating, probing and integrating a diversity of complex information, analytical findings and previous analyses; evaluating methodology, assessing potential biases, challenging findings, determining overall reliability and accuracy. Typically relates to program or service area issues or improvements, or overall approach to broad program design or delivery or "case treatment".
At D6: Investigating complex data and information, identifying linkages and interplay and challenging existing ways of thinking; conceptualization and forward thinking; analyses covering several programs or service areas that are integrated and evaluated.

Definition - Case treatment: The development of an overall approach to how various categories of cases or situations are handled.

ELEMENT 3 - COMMUNICATION SKILLS (application guidelines):
Cumulative progression element.
At D1: Convey simple, factual messages with limited requirement to modify or explain information; straightforward processes.
At D2: Retrieve or obtain information that is typically readily available; compose original content for straightforward communication materials.
At D3: Question clients to obtain additional information to uncover facts not readily available; influence and persuade individual clients on specific cases or issues; consult with relevant parties to discuss or provide information on specific program/operational delivery matters.
At D4: Influence and negotiate broad program initiatives; subject matter typically horizontal and broad-based; consulting and facilitating discussions with parties with different views on program/service/operational issues.
At D5: Present very complex arguments and strategic positions or issues, often new or innovative concepts to audiences not always receptive; extensive communication skills to officially present, defend, or advocate the Agency's strategic position on behalf of the organisation as a whole.

ELEMENT 4 - INTERACTION (application guidelines):
Pyramid element.
At D1: Routine or non-routine information that is straightforward, typically easy to understand and/or directly linked to work area.
At D2: Provide technical information or direction to resolve a client problem, advance an issue or provide background information; scope of problem/issue typically straightforward and routine procedures applied.
At D3: Problems or situations more difficult due to sensitivity of issues or parties where objectives tend to differ and information is not forthcoming; purpose is to dig and investigate for compliance, or to bring parties together and advance or resolve issues.
At D4: Providing high-level advice at the service, program or policy level; issues often sensitive; key differentiator from D3 is the breadth of consultation needed with business partners on program/policy development requirements and their implementation.
At D5: Expert level management advice on contentious issues with positions in conflict; high-level consultation with industry, stakeholders to advance broad Agency issues, interests and values; scope and nature of issues must be of significant importance to the Agency.

ELEMENT 5 - PEOPLE AND OPERATIONAL MANAGEMENT (application guidelines):
Distinction between supervision (administration of pre-defined activities) and management (deciding/defining what the activities are):
At D1: Providing information or expertise to team members through leading short-term or ad hoc work teams; guidance on how to deliver work, monitoring progress, providing feedback on results.
At D2: Ongoing full-time responsibility for formal people supervision; developing work plans, monitoring progress from plans and conducting performance evaluation; adjusting priorities, procedures, workflow and human resources.
At D3: Management of a program or service area; may include managing areas with front line supervision; dealing with a variety of stakeholders; broader range of planning; increased responsibility for planning, goal setting and controlling human and financial resources.
At D4 and D5: Management role grows to responsibility for providing leadership through a subordinate layer of management (not including front line supervisors); high-degree of control on resources and accountability for management of programs and services Agency-wide. Key difference: D5 has more complex subordinate operations with more breadth from program or service level to highest responsibility for Agency-wide issues.

ELEMENT 6 - DECISION MAKING (application guidelines):
At D1: Decisions typically issue specific and straightforward; guidance typically available from superiors.
At D2: Decisions relate primarily to ensuring team operational tasks are met; limited scope; decisions constrained by established procedures.
At D3: Some latitude in determining approach; decisions based on guidelines, regulations and established precedents.
At D4: Significant latitude in determining approach; considerable judgment and interpretation required for diverse and complex cases.
At D5: Relates to organization and coordination of program service objectives; required to manage independently.
At D6: Relates to determination of approaches to program development within a variety of integrated operations.
At D7: Relates to establishment and achievement of broad operational objectives.

ELEMENT 7 - PHYSICAL EFFORT (application guidelines):
Intensity should be considered first (Light, Moderate or Heavy), then Frequency (Rarely, Occasionally, Regularly). Select the single highest normal level of physical effort that applies to the job. Ensure the effort is a formal requirement of the job.

ELEMENT 8 - SENSORY EFFORT (application guidelines):
Frequency is defined as how often the job is expected to perform the effort. The assumption is that the sensory effort is performed on a prolonged basis when it occurs.

ELEMENT 9 - RISK TO HEALTH (application guidelines):
Captures only risks remaining after the application of normal safety precautions. Measures inherent risk remaining in the work assuming all safety measures are in place and all appropriate practices are followed.

ELEMENT 10 - WORK ENVIRONMENT (application guidelines):
Both components (Psychological and Physical) must be rated separately. Assumes the organization is in compliance with all current legislation and standards governing the work environment. Do not consider inefficiencies of heating, cooling and ventilation systems.

EXAMPLES OF WORK ACTIVITIES (selected samples):

Element 1 - Knowledge:
D2 EWA: Requires knowledge of practices and methodologies associated with administration related to case management and file processing, and administrative decision making to assemble, and process case documentation and documentary evidence related to files/cases.
D3 EWA: Requires understanding of the application and intent of legislation and regulations as they relate to the delivery of CBSA program(s); understanding of technical concepts and principals, as well as knowledge of legislation, regulations, policy and procedures in order to support day-to-day enforcement activities.
D4 EWA: Requires knowledge of programs and policies and service standards and an understanding of the intent of governing legislation, regulations and operating guidelines and principles to develop and/or fine tune policies and guidelines for use by others.
D5 EWA: Requires knowledge of the principles and practices of management in order to manage an operational area and achieve operational objectives and establish the business direction for that operational area.
D6 EWA: Requires a strategic level of knowledge of the intelligence field and sub-disciplines; knowledge of military affairs and conflict; knowledge of events, culture, socio-economic situations; uses knowledge to enable agency to achieve its mandate.
